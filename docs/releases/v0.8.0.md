# Desk v0.8.0

The defensible decision record is complete on the ADR-0010 line: the project Desk was started on can be given its signing key through the upgrade offer, a trail whose last line is incomplete can be repaired on the owner's word, each desk's trail can be stamped with a time-stamping authority the owner sets, and the README and in-app help describe the whole. A cross-vendor audit of the line as a composition then found seven interactions between the parts that no per-change review could see; a second audit after those fixes found five more, and a third pass found seven more in the same two decision tables — when a recovery, a creation sweep or a live rotation may remove a signing key. So this release replaces those tables with one rule: **Desk never removes a signing key on its own.** A key it cannot prove superseded is archived under its custody and named on the decision record; the owner removes it on their word. A fourth pass over the new rule found three edges in its new code, fixed in #335; a fifth pass re-ran every scenario of all four passes and both rounds against the result: every one holds. Beside that line, four topics ported from a working copy: named AI connections for the assistant, search steps with a research envelope and a files editor, Admin grouped by task, and document processing (OCR) through the gateway. The pinned Gateway moves to v0.10.0; Runtime 0.27.1 and Runner 0.6.0 are unchanged.

**Updating from v0.7.0:** the first start writes an identity file for the project Desk was started on (`<project>/.desk-private/project.json`) and takes a project-scoped lock for the upgrade; nothing changes a desk's `jpack.json`, a Jobs store or a signing key without the owner's confirmation on the page. Updating from v0.5.x or earlier: see [v0.6.0's notes](https://github.com/Judgment-Pack/judgment-pack-desk/blob/v0.6.0/docs/releases/v0.6.0.md) and back up every desk's Jobs store first.

## What changes for you

- **The startup project's key** (Admin → Decision safeguards). The project Desk was started on, which has no desk of its own, is offered its signing key through the upgrade: Desk shows the exact `jpack.json` bytes it will write and the key it will create, and writes them only on your confirmation, one transaction at a time. A copy of a project (two folders, one identity) gets no recovery, key or rotation, and the page says why.
- **Repair** (Decision record → Repair). When a trail's last line is incomplete, Desk shows the runtime's own report and the exact repair it would ask for, and runs `audit repair` only on your word; the decision record then shows the repaired trail's identity and the segments.
- **Stamping** (Decision record → Stamping). You name a time-stamping authority (its URL and roots); Desk stamps the trail's checkpoints on the interval you set and checks the stamps with the runtime's verifier. The panel says which stamps the runtime checked and which are only the authority's answer to Desk's request.
- **The decision record's sentences and counts** are held to what was passed to the runtime: the headline names keys and checkpoints only where they were passed; "records since" a hand-over and "records pending a stamp" are the runtime's own counts, and are called lines where the report gives no count.
- **Named AI connections** (Admin → Connections & access → Connections › AI). Each assistant provider is a named connection on this computer; each desk chooses one. A key is entered once and never shown again.
- **Search and files.** Search steps show their sources with attribution; a web-search timeout can be set once the gateway advertises its bounds; Project files has an editor, and the files API refuses to write any file named like an audit record.
- **Admin grouped by task:** Workspace, Connections & access, Decision safeguards. The old links still resolve.
- **Document processing (OCR)** (Admin → Connections & access → Document processing). While the desk's local gateway runs, a **Scanned pages** part sets the OCR mode (Off, the default, or When a page has no text), the processor and a processing timeout of 10 to 120 seconds, and manages the processors on this computer: local Tesseract, an OCR program in the gateway's OCR tools bundle or `/usr/bin`, Google Document AI, Azure Document Intelligence or Amazon Textract. A cloud credential is entered once and never shown again. **Test a PDF** runs one PDF through a saved processor and shows a short preview, keeping nothing. Turning OCR on or off restarts the local gateway, and the page says so before the save ("Save and restart") and says which plan the running gateway has. A processor whose programs are not installed is marked, and turning OCR on with it says that every document read stops until they are. A desk that uses another gateway is not offered these settings.
- **Sign-in** draws its link, "Cancel sign-in" and the end of the progress line from the login's answer (#258).

## What the record establishes, and what it does not

Unchanged from v0.7.0 and quoted verbatim where the pages quote the ADR: "Desk ran this on your machine, over your trail, with keys and checkpoints you keep. It shows what a holder would see. It is not evidence to anyone who does not trust you. A holder runs the same command on a copy, with what it holds." Where no key was passed, the headline now says so ("with checkpoints you keep and no public key"). A stamp establishes "the checkpoint, and every line before it, existed by the authority's stated time, as far as that authority is independent of the operator", and not when any record was made, nor anything against an authority that colludes, nor anything after the last checkpoint stamped.

## The startup project's key through the upgrade (#261, ADR-0010 row 4; #296)

- The offer shows the bytes it writes and the key it creates; the confirmation token binds both and is spent on use.
- The project is named by an identity it keeps (`.desk-private/project.json`), not by its path's hash; a copy is detected and refused recovery, a key and rotation. The upgrade runs under a project-scoped lock, refuses without it, and puts a lock back only over a lock of its own configuration; a failed upgrade removes the key before the identity, so no state is left with a key and no owner.

## Repair on the owner's word (#263, ADR-0010 row 6)

- Desk shows `audit verify`'s report and the exact `audit repair` it would run; the token binds the trail's identity and the damaged line's digest, so a trail that changed since is refused as stale.
- A repaired trail's identity moves every cursor as the ADR says; hand-over and stamping count the records after a repair from the runtime's report, one record where a subtraction over physical lines said two (#294).

## Stamping with a time-stamping authority (#274, ADR-0010 row 7; #294)

- `audit stamp --tsa <url> --timeout 15s` with the roots, policy and revocation lists you set; the scheduler stamps only a head that is not the checkpoint it last knew stamped (trail, sequence and record digest), so a trail restored to an earlier point and written since is stamped again.
- The panel counts a run's checkpoint as checked only by stamps of its own trail reaching its sequence; otherwise it stays "the authority's answer to Desk's request, not a stamp the runtime checked".

## Key rotation recovery across a changed trail (#302, ADR-0010 line audit findings 3–4)

- The rotation marker is a journal: one line with the phase (`generate`, `rotate`, `finish`), the trail the rotation was made on, the next key and the sequence, written whole and synced before each step. A rotation obtains its trail before anything is made (the sidecar's, or `audit checkpoint`'s before the trail's first signature) and refuses with no chained record, so a journal never names no trail. Where `finish` cannot be written, the rotation stops before the list names the next key.
- A start decides from the journal and from the sidecar of the trail it names alone. At `rotate` or `finish`, a sidecar that is gone, names another trail or names two decides nothing: both seeds and the list are kept, and the decision record says so. A `finish` the sidecar does not hand over to, or a list that already names the next key, is unknown, never undone. A seed is never removed because a read failed or while a sidecar line names it.
- Each key a rotation adds carries the trail it was made on in the key list (never shown). After a trail is moved aside, the next record starts a new trail signed by the key Desk kept: the decision record shows that key as the new trail's first, passes it alone to `audit verify` (measured with runtime 0.27.1: the earlier trail's keys make it report the new trail invalid), and offers the next rotation. A key's sequence must follow the previous key's only on the same trail.
- Recovery resolves a desk by its own signing identity, so a desk Desk made and opened directly finishes its own stopped rotation; it never does so under an identity another folder holds (the copy-versus-move rule of #296, now for made desks too). Where the signing lock is held, recovery waits once (up to 10 seconds) and otherwise leaves the markers for the next start and says so. Each resumed desk's Runner starts only after that recovery.

## Desk never removes a signing key on its own (#327; ADR-0010 amended)

- Where Desk once removed a seed, a next seed or a public-key list — a rotation's recovery, the creation sweep, a failed upgrade, a list that could not be published — it now **archives** the file under `<configuration>/secrets/signing/archive/<identity>/`, owner-only like the keys, with one journal line written first and a sentence on the decision record saying which rule archived it and what Desk could not decide.
- A rotation promotes the next key by archiving the previous seed first and renaming the next into its place; the public-key list is appended, never rewritten. A trail replaced during that moment leaves both seeds present, one live and one archived, and the conflict named; it never leaves a seed lost.
- The decision record lists every archived entry (identity, trail, sequence, when, why) with **Remove**, confirmed with a token that binds the entry and the file's bytes and is spent once used. Desk removes nothing without it.
- A project whose identity Desk cannot settle (a copy, a folder moved and reused, an identity file lost) is *unresolved*: it signs and rotates nothing until you resolve it on the decision record ("moved here" or "a copy"), with a token that binds what the former folder holds. The creation sweep archives only the leftovers of a creation that provably never published. No key changes on any failure to take the signing lock.
- For a stamp run older than the head, "a stamp the runtime checked" is decided by one `audit verify --expect` with the run's own checkpoint, counted only on `valid`, and kept only under the head the runtime verified.

What it establishes: nothing more than before about a record — it changes what Desk does to its own keys. What it costs: a folder of archived seeds on a machine that stopped mid-rotation, which the owner clears on their word.

## The ported topics

- **AI connections (#276):** connections are kept under Desk's custody like keys (owner-only files in an owner-only folder, never in a project, never on the page after entry, read back and held to what was written); the registry's save holds its lock only across the write.
- **Search, the research relay and files (#293):** the read-only decision for audit-named files is made on the resolved path, the name and the mode and on `jpack.json` as read at that moment, case-insensitively and against hard links; an unreadable `jpack.json` keeps every such file read-only; the attribution frame carries `allow-same-origin` without `allow-scripts`.
- **Admin by task (#301):** every section and panel of v0.7.0 is kept, the decision record, the Jobs record, hand-over, repair and stamping among them.
- **Document processing (#304):** `POST /api/document-processing/{status,configure,test}` goes only to this desk's own local gateway companion, refusing when another gateway is in use and when the URL has a query; answers to the page are rebuilt from the fields Desk names, so no answer carries a credential, and an answer that contains the credential a save carried is refused whole; the request is bounded before anything is written. `--local-plan` now receives the connections directory, and a source's longer envelope (150 s for the four document sources, 130 s for web search) is taken only with the flag the gateway advertises it under, all four or none, else the plan is refused whole. The relay gives a document read its longer deadline only while the running plan gives processing. The component lock pins gateway v0.10.0 by version and revision (the tag's peeled commit), and the packaging holds the archives to the release's checksums and attestation; the bundle carries the two OCR workers, and the local-gateway check runs an end-to-end walk against the published binaries in CI.

## Also in this release

- The mutation harness runs each row against the tests that can notice it, with a whole-suite fallback (#256); a pull request's own rows run in CI from the harness diff (#277); the nightly runs only when main changed, in ten shards, and opens and closes its own survivor issue (#268, #295); rows that failed by a hang or a panic now fail by assertion (#259, #270, #298); the records of what caught each row are committed (#267, #280).
- `i18n:check`: a key missing from a translated catalogue is a warning with an English fallback; `--strict` keeps the old refusal (#278). New strings in this release are English in every catalogue until the translation pass.
- The README and in-app help describe the decision record end to end (#281).

## Verification

CI ran frontend behaviour, all twelve locale catalogues, the Go chassis tests, the Jobs companion against the pinned Runner and Runtime, the local Gateway checks against the pinned Gateway v0.10.0 on ubuntu and macOS, each pull request's own mutation rows, and the native archive builds. The nightly mutation run over the whole record was clean on 6127c40, the commit this release is cut from (apart from these notes) (2,550 rows).

- **Line audits:** the first cross-vendor audit of the ADR-0010 composition (over `main` at 2ce71df) found seven HIGH interactions and two MEDIUM (#283–#291, tracked in #292), fixed in #296, #302 and #294, each with its own round; the second (over 25c2815) found five more (#309–#313), fixed in #315 and #317; the third (over 0fc5744) found seven more in the same tables (#319–#325), answered by the archive rule in #327, whose own round found seven defects in the new code, fixed before merge. A fourth pass (over 9075b7b) found six defects at the rule's edges (#329–#334), fixed in #335 with its own round; a fifth pass (over ae81e6a) re-ran every probe of the four passes and the rounds: all pass, no regression, verdict SOUND for the rule. Not exercised by the auditor's sandbox: socket-dependent fixtures and real-runtime key generation, which run on CI.
- **Review:** each change to keys, confirmations, hand-over bytes or verification claims had one review round, recorded on its pull request with a disposition for every finding: #261 (2 HIGH, 1 MEDIUM fixed), #263 (4 HIGH fixed or mitigated; runtime #230 filed), #274 (2 MEDIUM, 5 LOW fixed; runtime #231 filed), #276 (3 MEDIUM, 3 LOW fixed), #293 (1 HIGH, 1 MEDIUM, 4 LOW fixed), #294 (1 HIGH fixed; #299, #300 filed), #296 (3 HIGH fixed), #302 (3 HIGH, 1 MEDIUM fixed), #304 (3 MEDIUM, 1 LOW fixed; #305–#307 filed), #315 (4 HIGH, 2 MEDIUM fixed), #317 (3 MEDIUM, 3 LOW fixed; #318 filed), #327 (3 HIGH, 4 MEDIUM fixed), #335 (1 HIGH, 2 MEDIUM, 1 LOW fixed).
- **Mutation checks:** every safeguard added here has a row in `scripts/mutation-expects.tsv`, caught by a named test; the tallies are in the pull requests.

Not exercised: a real time-stamping authority (the runtime's stand-in authority only); two Desk processes on one project; a real cloud OCR call or a real Tesseract run; `flock` on macOS; a real runtime stopped mid-rotation (stops are simulated at Desk's own pause points).

Open by decision: #318 (what "checked" means against the run's own token), #290 and #291 (the line audit's two MEDIUM findings), #262 (rotation offered for a made desk whose `jpack.json` names a copy of the seed), #282 (agent setup with the key), ADR-0010 row 10 (the HTTPS hand-over endpoint, which waits for a holder), #299, #300, #303.
